Released in 1984, "Somebody's Watching Me" is the ultimate anthem of 80s synth-pop paranoia. It managed to blend a spooky, minor-key atmosphere with a danceable groove that made it an instant club hit and a permanent fixture on Halloween playlists.

---

### **The Song: A Portrait of Paranoia**
The track is built on a foundation of "paranoid pop." It captures the anxiety of the early 80s—a time when technology was becoming more invasive and the Cold War was still casting a shadow over daily life.

* **The Groove:** The song features a heavy, rhythmic bassline and sharp, staccato synthesizers. It draws heavily from the New Wave and Post-Disco movements.
* **The Hook:** The chorus is arguably one of the most recognizable in pop history. It provides a melodic, soulful contrast to the jittery energy of the verses.
* **The Lyrics:** The lyrics describe mundane activities—taking a shower, watching TV, walking home—but infuse them with the terrifying sensation of being observed by an unknown entity.

### **The Singers' Styles**
The song is actually a fascinating vocal "duet" between two very different styles, which is why it works so well.

#### **Rockwell (Kennedy William Gordy)**
Rockwell’s style on this track is **theatrically anxious**. 
* **The "Spoken-Sung" Delivery:** Rather than traditional soulful singing, Rockwell uses a rhythmic, almost frantic "sprechgesang" (spoken-singing) style. 
* **The Pseudo-British Accent:** Interestingly, Rockwell adopted a slight, stylized mid-Atlantic/British accent for the verses. He later explained this was an attempt to sound more like the New Wave artists he admired and to distance his sound from the "Motown royalty" legacy of his father, Berry Gordy.
* **The Persona:** He plays the "Everyman" pushed to the brink of a nervous breakdown.

#### **The Uncredited Feature: Michael Jackson**
While Rockwell handled the verses, the chorus was sung by his childhood friend, **Michael Jackson**.
* **Smooth Contrast:** MJ’s vocal is smooth, soaring, and incredibly melodic. It acts as the "inner voice" or the ghostly manifestation of the paranoia that Rockwell is feeling.
* **The "Jackson" Touch:** Even without being credited on the original label, his signature vibrato and soulful precision made the song an international smash. It’s the "sugar" that helps the "medicine" of the dark lyrics go down.

---

### **Fun Fact: A Family Affair**
Rockwell didn't want his father (the head of Motown Records) to know he was making music, fearing favoritism. He recorded the demo in private, but once Berry Gordy heard the finished product—especially with MJ on the hook—he knew they had a massive hit on their hands.
